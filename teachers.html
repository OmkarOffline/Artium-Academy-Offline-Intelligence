Cohort Renewals
—
Cohort
Every learner whose membership ends in that month.
Counted under the teacher who taught that membership.
Renewed
Learners in the cohort who have renewed so far.
Counts whenever they paid: early, on time or late.
Keeps rising as late payers renew, so older months can still change.
Renewal %
How well the teacher held on to that group of learners.
Worked out as
Renewed ÷ cohort
Last 3M, 6M, 12M and Lifetime
All the cohorts in those months put together.
Worked out as
Renewed ÷ due, completed months only
This month is left out until it's over.
Recent cohorts are still renewing, so the last 3 months usually read lower than 12 months. That alone does not mean the teacher has slipped.
Due, Renewed, % Renewed and Yet to renew
Memberships ending from January to the end of this month.
% Renewed = Renewed ÷ Due
Due: how many were due.
Renewed: how many have renewed so far.
Yet to renew: how many have not renewed yet.
Will not renew
Learners who have not renewed and are marked "Will Not Renew".
Opportunity
The learners to reach out to.
Worked out as
Yet to renew − Will not renew
Learners who have not renewed and have not said no.

Collection efficiency
How much of what fell due we actually collected.
Worked out as
Collected ÷ due

All current teachers together, the same as the Total row below.
Due
Memberships whose original package expiry date falls in the period.
An extension does not move a membership to another period.
Counted under the teacher who taught them.
Collected
Renewals whose date of renewal falls in the period.
Includes
✓Learners due in the period who paid on time.
✓Win-backs: learners due earlier who paid late.
✓Pre-renewals: learners who paid in advance.
Date of renewal is column A of the Renewals tab.
Each period stands on its own
A payment counts in the period it was made.
1A learner due in September who pays in October counts in October.
2September stays as it was, so months compare fairly.
The chart
Every period side by side.
1MoM, QoQ, YoY: by month, quarter or year.
23M to All: how many months to show.
3Click a bar to pick that month, quarter or year.
The pink line is the average of the finished periods shown: everything collected ÷ everything due.
The dashed line is the trend: whether the periods shown are rising or falling.
Gold, silver and bronze mark the three best periods shown.
The striped bar is the current period. It is not ranked until it ends.
Trend
Which way collection efficiency is moving, and how fast.
For example
▲ 0.4 pts a month
Rising by about 0.4 percentage points every month.
Over 12 months that adds up to about 5 points: a typical 70% month a year ago is a typical 75% month now.
How it is worked out
The dashed line that best fits every bar shown.
1It looks at all the finished bars, not just the first and the last.
2So one very good or very bad month does not swing it much.
3Its slope is the pts a month figure.
The current month is left out until it ends.
Average and trend together
Two different questions.
1Average: how good are we, typically?
2Trend: are we getting better or worse, and how fast?
A good average with a ▼ trend means we are slipping.
How big is big
Reading the number.
Under 0.5 pts: steady, not really changing.
About 1 to 2 pts: clear, steady improvement.
More than that: a big shift. Check whether one standout month is behind it.
▼ in red: we are slipping.
Pick the window
3M, 6M, 9M, 12M or All change what it covers.
13M: recent momentum, useful after a new push.
212M or All: the longer direction.
3M ▲ with 12M ▼ means things have started turning around.
